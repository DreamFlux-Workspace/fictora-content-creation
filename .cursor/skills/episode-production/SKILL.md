# Episode production

You are the agent. The human is the gate. The desk folder is the review surface.

**Repo:** `fictora-content-creation` only. **API:** deployed Drama Generation `/v1/*`. **Tools:** `uv run fictora-produce` (the stages) and `uv run fictora-ops` (desk, ledger, notes). `--help` on any command is the truth for its flags.

Read when you need the detail, not all on the first turn:

- Gate stop cards: [checklists.md](../../../docs/content-ops/checklists.md)

## Two rules

1. **A human says yes before money moves.** The pitch card (the idea), the look (once you drew a look frame), plates, voices, script, board, the spend yes. One gate per yes. No batched gates. Silence is not consent; a yes from last time does not carry forward.
2. **Render once.** Every paid unit is rendered once. A second render needs a written cause naming what in the direction produced the fault. "Try again" is not a cause.

## Desks created before 6 Oct 2026 keep their original behaviour

New rules apply to new desks (user decision, 6 Oct 2026, "only for new desks"). Exceptions that only stop a second charge apply to every desk (founder, 7 Oct 2026): a re-run after a lost reply sends the same request (the server hands back the job it already has), and a dropped connection while `step` waits never marks the desk failed (run the same command again). A desk created before 6 Oct 2026 (local date) produces exactly what it produced before, bug and safety fixes included: its reels stay flat in `reels/` (they now get the cover image and its first frame, founder 7 Oct 2026), no draft names, no `latest.json` or `metrics.csv` row and no posting notes, and are made only by `reel` (no automatic reel after `finish` or an edit); its post text says "Series · Episode N" and "Episode N+1 is next. Follow so you don't miss it."; its genre words, cold-open warning and face detectors are the older ones; it never draws system panels (finish or reel); its captions keep the line-window spreading, the wording it always had, their dashes, and `house` (Subtle) when no style is set, and it is never asked for the caption preview. Everything else on this page is for new desks.

- How a desk is placed: `rules_epoch` in `production.config.json` (`start` stamps `"2026-10-06"` on every new desk), else `series.json` `day`, else the folder's date prefix (`2026-09-26-…`). No date at all: the current rules.
- `uv run fictora-produce rules-epoch --desk D` prints the desk's epoch and why (free). Only on the human's say-so: `--set 2026-10-06` opts an older desk in to the current rules; `--set legacy` keeps a desk on its original behaviour.
- The legacy code paths are frozen for those desks: never change them.

## Never

- Chain two stages in one turn, or run two `step` calls in one turn unless the human asked to catch up.
- Approve plates, script or a board in the turn you enrolled them.
- Overwrite a desk file. New version, new `-vN` name (`fictora-ops next-path`). A desk that already exists for that date and slug is refused by `start`: never delete it or pick another folder to get round it; ask the human.
- Write your own HTTP around the API. Where no command exists it is a DEVIATION (reference.md) and a backlog line.
- Fetch, save, print or quote a compiled prompt (`GET /v1/jobs/{id}/provider-spec`). Record job ids instead (Prompt policy).
- Clone or read `fictora-drama`. Edit product code or settings to get a different look.
- Print or commit `FICTORA_DRAMA_GENERATION_SERVICE_TOKEN`.
- Retry a video job stuck in post. `cancel-job` it.
- Call hosted post-production, or pass `--api-captions`. Hosted post is off.
- Regenerate the story or re-film every take to change a voice.
- Hand over a raw take. A take is not done until the local finish ran.
- Copy a desk folder to try something. A copy keeps the same `spine_id`, so it is the same server story: an edit, redraw or film on the copy changes the producer's real story. The kit stops such a command and names the other desk (`Stopped: this desk's story (spine …) is also the story of: …`); pass `--shared-spine-ok` only when the human says both desks are meant to share it. Reads and local post (`status`, `spine`, `review`, `join`, `trim`, `reel`, `set-bed` …) are never stopped.

## Pitch card: the idea, approved before any paid draw

Write it with the producer before the draft, from the brief; it is what they say yes to, not the lines. Half of four series' spend (~$13.50 of ~$44, 30 Sep – 7 Oct 2026) went on re-renders after the idea was rejected late: "the romance feels forced", "the plot is all over the place", "she does not have any expression", "don't make up weird things this is bl", "sota cannot feel the presence of a ghost", "what do u keep bringing rain in?". The Gallery Heiress Tuesday (every rule below applied) was right on the first draw: $3.00 against Monday's $8.10.

Fields (`pitch.md`: one `## field` heading each; lists as `- ` bullets; an emotional beat is `- what happens | expression: named face | delivery: through_tears`; yes/no fields take `yes` or `no`): `throughline` (one sentence), `charged_situation`, `world_rules`, `premise_device` (`device:`, `used:`, `why:`), `emotional_beats`, `intimacy` (`none` or a PG plan), `shouted_lines`, `hook_line_on_screen`, `hook` (the frame-0 image in words), `ending`, `voices` (`mode:` and one `NAME: voice` line each; `pitch` prints what the desk knows: confirm it with the producer). `pitch` warns, free, on a restrained expression and on the premise device used two episodes running.

Check the card against these before you show it:

- **Expression and delivery on every emotional beat.** Name a big visible face (tears, rage, a trembling smile) and a delivery. Restraint (freeze, blank, stunned, a cold smile, deadpan on grief) films as a blank face.
- **PG through the aftermath.** Never a kiss, an embrace or touching lips (Fictora policy: never remove the frames' PG bans). The pair springing apart, lipstick smeared on his mouth, her thumb on her own lip, hands meeting on an umbrella handle.
- **One charged situation; romance in subtext; no invented prop business.** One throughline, not a plot turn every beat. No business the story did not ask for (a pole gripped, a lamp, pointing at a wall).
- **A line or a visible action every 3 to 4 s.** About 3 lines a take; beat 1 has a line and opens mid-motion.
- **Every line checked against what the speaker sees and does** ("weren't you closed?" from someone already eating; a new bowl when she has one).
- **World rules written at episode 1 and checked** in every pitch, beat and board row ("Sota cannot feel or touch a ghost").
- **The premise device used sparingly.** Each episode finds its own turn (the hospital call, not the rain again).
- **Faces fit the genre.** Never a comedy face (dot eyes, a beaming smile) on a grief or horror beat.
- **Ages as digits** on every cast card ("26", never "twenty-six"): a worded age was read as under 18 and fixing it redrew every plate.
- **Ask the producer** whether a shouted line is wanted and whether a hook line is burned on screen (`finish --no-hook-line`); never assume either.
- **About 10 syllables a beat** for Korean and Japanese lines (about 11 s of speech a take).

After the draft, `author` and `rewrite`, the script gate prints free warnings next to the other nudges (every desk): a take whose first beat has no line, a take with fewer than 3 lines, restraint words in a beat, an age written in words on a cast card, and, from the card, an emotional beat with no expression or no delivery on the story, the premise device in an episode whose card says it is not used (or in each of the last 3 episodes), and the world rules with "check every line and beat against these" (no automatic meaning check: read them).

## Brief

Pick the nearest `--preset-id` at `start` (`fictora-produce presets` lists the published ids; `start` checks the id before it makes the desk and a wrong one names the valid ids), even for our own look (Cold Gate `modern-dark-fantasy` is the closest to horror; the Dead Light horror preset cannot be picked yet). Without a pinned look frame its world leaks into plates, so pin the look frame or steer it with `look-note` before the plates are drawn.

Write it with the human (`docs/content-ops/templates/brief.md`) and check before the draft:

- **Premise:** a visible physical event with a small turn. Beats are what hands, bodies and objects do, in order.
- **Episode 1 alone.** Do not ask for a series arc; do not outline four or five episodes. The arc is chosen at episode 2.
- **Hook** (below) is in the first beat.
- **Lines:** three at most per 15 s take, one per beat. A 15 s take is three beats, so a silent comic beat (a pause, a stare, a freeze) costs a line: say so and let the human choose. Ask who each line is said TO. To lock lines word for word, write "Keep these lines exactly as written." inside the brief's Lines section and fill each line's beat and `### Take N`; the server then puts back a locked line the writers trimmed or reworded (reference.md, Script gate). When locked lines cannot fit, `step` prints `PAUSED for the creator`: edit the brief with them, `bind` it (or `brief --edit`), `step` again. The edited brief is a new draft; the same brief only shows the pause again (nothing sent).
- **Silent episodes are allowed:** an episode or beat may have no lines. A silent TAKE still needs a visible action every 3 to 4 s, and its first beat opens mid-motion. A sound in the brief (a hiss, a scream, "only rain") is sound, never a line; if the draft still shows one as a line, remove it with `line --remove` (a character the beats move is never a stranded voice).
- **New characters from episode 2:** a direction or note that names a new person who speaks or is seen gets them a cast card (look and voice in the series' style). `author` prints `new character added: NAME — approve their picture before boards`; after the script yes, `step` draws only their picture (~$0.30), the human approves it with `approve --gate plates`, then boards. A story holds four characters: past that the server refuses by name (`new_cast_over_limit`) and gives nobody their lines. Crowds stay unnamed (below).
- **Unnamed extras** (a new crew, guards, a crowd): write each one a look of their own in the brief, or in the redraw note once the board is drawn: a different face, hair and build, their own stencil or name tag. Never leave them as "the crew": the board draws one shared face (often the lead's, stencil and all), and the take may then show the same person twice. Name extras by look, not by designation: a note saying "D-7 kneels" or "guard 2 at the door" cannot reach the right figure until the server fix (an unnamed-figures field) ships, so write "the tall bald man in the orange jumpsuit kneels" instead.
- **A new character who is seen, mid-season:** `line --add --beat N --text "…" --new-character NAME --role "…" --voice-description "…" --look @look.txt [--staging '{"frame_position": …, "pose": …, "gaze": …, "interaction": …}']` adds them, gives them a look, puts the line on screen with them moving and stages them in the beat's frame (four edits; `--staging` is required when the beat is already drawn). Everything is checked before the first edit; a run that stops says what was done and is finished by running the same command again. Then their plate (`redraw-plate`, printed). `--new-voice` stays for someone only heard; give it `--look` too if they may be drawn later.
- **One-sided features name their side.** A prosthetic or chrome limb, a scar, a burn, an eyepatch, a tattoo, a single earring or an injury is written with the character's own side ("her own left forearm"); without it the plate can draw it on the wrong side or on both (Three Payments Late 10-05: two chrome arms, a $0.30 plate redraw). After the draft and before the plates the kit prints `!! NAME: "…" names no side` (warning only): fix the card with `cast --desk D --name NAME --look @look.txt` before `step` draws the plates. The server adds the mirror and pair bans ("a chrome right forearm on Noor", "two chrome forearms") and, for an adult on a drawn style, adult face words to the card itself (fictora-drama).
- **A cast member with no look** (a voice a later episode puts in a frame; `redraw-plate` refused `cast_visual_brief_missing`): `cast --desk D --name NAME --look @look.txt` (`--preview` first after the script gate), then the `redraw-plate` it prints. A look file is `key: value` lines: age, gender, face, hair, silhouette, wardrobe (lists split on `;`), optional expression, gaze, posture, never; any other line is the description. The drawing style comes from a cast member who already has a look.
- **Narrator lines in the brief:** `start` and the draft `step` print `!! the brief has N narrator / voice-over line(s)` and the lines. Voice-over is laid dry in post, never written into the take: with the human, either keep them (they become a heard-only character's lines) or run `brief --desk D --strip-narration` before the first `step`. A warning, never a stop.
- **One-person shows:** a server that reports `cast_floor` 1 (`GET /v1/capabilities`) is asked for exactly the people the story needs, so a story about one person is drafted with one. An older server still needs two, and the draft asks for a heard-only voice or a second character the story needs, never a narrator invented to reach two.
- **Narrator-named characters:** a cast member named like a narrator (Narrator, Storyteller, Voice-over, VO, Inner voice) is asked about once: `NAME — heard only, never seen? [y/N]`. Ask the human; never decide from the name. `step`, `author` and `line --new-voice` stop when nobody can answer: pass `--narrator-heard-only NAME` (their lines go off screen, no plate) or `--narrator-on-screen NAME` (an ordinary drawn character). The answer is kept in `narrator-answers.json`. An off-screen person who is not named like a narrator is never asked and never becomes a narrator.
- **Off-screen voices:** declare as voice only; never drawn, not even a sleeve or a shadow. A line whose frame or beat keeps its speaker out of view ("not visible" staging, "visible NAME" forbidden on the beat) is heard off screen for that line only, flag or not; `line` lists it as `(off-screen)`. A character's own thoughts are not a voice: they go on that character with `inner-voice`, never a `--new-voice` (it takes one of four cast places). Over another character's face it reads as that face speaking: give it a source (a wall grille, a phone in hand) or a post treatment.
- **Age:** any age; write the real age. Nobody under 18 in romance, ever (reference.md), and no child in a romantic, sexual or intimate scene, not even as a bystander: the server refuses such a beat or frame as `minor_in_intimate_scene` (authoring, `edit`/`line`, boards, estimate, film; nothing drawn or paid) and the kit names the episode, beat or frame, the child and the words, and the fix: move the child out of that shot, or change the scene. Never age the child up to keep it. Children on camera (P0): camera at or above the child's eye level, never below; falls and landings feet-first or seated with legs together; leggings or shorts under coats and dresses. Check the take, not just the board, before captioning (runbook, Characters and age).
- **Stylised styles only, said out loud (founder rule, 1 Oct 2026).** Fictora draws only its published anime/manhwa presets. A brief asking for photoreal, realistic, live-action or a real-film look is never converted quietly: the draft pauses (nothing sent) and names the presets and the closest one; pick one with the human (`bind --preset-id`), then `step --accept-notice style_not_available`. A look frame or look note must stay inside the stylised family too: `look-frame` and `look-note` print a WARNING on photoreal words (Sighted and Last Stream used a photoreal look frame; do not repeat it).
- **No real people, not even stylised (founder rule, 1 Oct 2026).** A named or recognisable real person (an actor, an idol, a public figure, a private person named as real) is never drawn or voiced, and no voice is matched to one. Archetypes are fine ("a K-pop idol type", "a cold CEO"). A brief that names one ("looks like <actor>", "an idol like <member>") pauses; a name the brief lists in its own cast (its `## Cast` table or list) is a character, not a real person, so "Ends on Dez's face." with Dez in the cast does not pause; with the human's yes, `step --accept-notice real_person_not_allowed` lets the writer make an original character with the same vibe, shown at the cast gate. The server may raise the same notices (`409 brief_needs_creator_choice`); the desk shows its words and pauses the same way.
- **Language:** a Japanese- or Korean-voiced show starts with `start --language ja` (or `ko`), so `spoken_language` is right from the draft. JA/KO lines must sound native in that situation; pin performed lines; a dialect needs a native speaker's yes. A show drafted in the wrong language is changed with `language --desk D --spoken ja|ko|en`: it prints what the server sets aside first (every performed line, subtitle and gloss written for the old language, localized again at script approval or before the next take; seed voice clips made again; takes filmed in another language), then sends it; `--preview` sends nothing; takes already filmed in another language refuse it until the human's yes (`--confirm-filmed`). Read the last line: `Applied` or `Refused: <reason>`. After it, `line --spoken` pins lines as on any Japanese show, and `language` prints the `line --spoken` command for each line recorded on the desk while the server held the show as en-US (`shared/spoken-language.json`). Against a server without the route, `line --spoken … --language ja` still records the line on the desk and sends nothing (exit 2); tell the human the take still speaks the English `--text`. Captions are English only for now: every JA/KO line needs an English subtitle (`line --spoken … --subtitle …`); the caption font has no Japanese or Korean glyphs, so a line whose caption (`subtitle_text`, else `text`) is not English is left uncaptioned and `finish` / `caption` print `NOT ENGLISH: <line id> "…"` (fix with `line --subtitle` / `edit`, then finish again).
- **Source material:** name its licence and credit (SCP is CC BY-SA 3.0: credit the author). Never copy the look of an image that is not under that licence. A creature inspired by a reference is written as a new original design.
- **Named characters:** each gets a clearly different outfit and silhouette; no stock names ("Mara Voss"). Jackets and uniforms: "plain back, no lettering or logos".
- **Shot plan and acting** (runbook, "Shot plan per take"): every shot names its camera move with direction and size ("camera holds" only on an insert); acting written big and physical; horror never one held shot or a fixed wide (the scare gets an insert or POV, the threat comes toward the camera). At most four board rows, so at most four shots a take.

## Hook: the first three seconds

- Frame 0 is chosen by genre. **Action, power fantasy, war-god, apocalypse, sci-fi, mystery and horror** may open on the strongest image (spectacle, a power-up, a transformation, an impact, the threat): no face required. **Romance, CEO, family, comedy and slice of life** open mid-motion on a face (a hand already moving, a head already turning). Either way never an establishing wide, never a still. A silent opening carried by the picture is allowed (below).
- The first line lands by about 0.5 s. A silent opening is allowed when the picture carries the hook (power shown, an emotional close-up, an impossible image).
- First line 10 words or fewer; no backstory in it.
- The reveal (what the episode is about) is on screen or said by about 3 s, after the commitment, never before it. When it must land by 3 s, keep the first line to about 2 s: a 4-second first line pushes the reveal to 4 s.
- Lines 10 words or fewer; a take about 22 spoken words or fewer.
- The last beat lands one new fact, never a fade. A quiet genre may close softly.
- Never a line addressed to the viewer (a call to action, "what would you do?"). One exception: a **POV episode**, where a character speaks to the camera as if it were the viewer (a brief that opens "POV: …", e.g. "POV: your ex shows up at your wedding"), keeps its lines to the camera. That is the story, not a call to action: it is still never "comment below" or a question about the show.
- At most one spectacle beat per take; none is fine.

The server writer already follows these. The word counts come back as nudges (fictora-drama #538), printed after the draft, `author`, `line` / `edit` and a cascade preview as `note: beat 3 line runs 14 words (10 recommended) — …` under `authoring notes (nudges: nothing was blocked or changed)`, and added to `run-notes.md`. They never block, never exit non-zero, and nothing is shortened: tell the human, and the creator decides. An older server sends none.

A board that opens on a wide or a pause is redrawn with that as the shape note, before any take. A weak open is never fixed by re-filming.

## Social safe zones (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

On 9:16 the platforms cover the **top 8%**, the **bottom 20%**, and the **right 12% of the lower two thirds**.

- Faces, eyes, mouths and key props never sit there. Everything else is free; do not centre faces by default.
- Captions sit in the band **55–70%** of the height.
- The Sokii mark goes **top left**, just under the top strip (23:121 on 768×1344, 0.6 opacity). Never top right.

## Board gate

Open the newest `boards/` file and check (full list: [checklists.md](../../../docs/content-ops/checklists.md)):

- **Visible cause:** every action row follows a visible face reacting to its cause, or shares the frame with it.
- **Each row opens on the emotion the row before it ended on.**
- **Speakers:** a spoken line sits on a row where its speaker is in frame, medium or closer. No off-screen speaker is drawn.
- **Cast list matches the words:** a cell whose moment names one character but whose cast list has another (Hanakaze ep 4: "Catches Ren..." listing Genzō) draws the listed one. The shot list prints `!! ... names X but lists Y`; edit that frame's cast, then redraw. The same check runs **before** boards are paid for: right after the draft and at the script yes it prints `!! Before the boards: …`, and `step` (the boards draw) and `redraw-board` stop with `!! Stopped before drawing the boards: nothing was sent or paid`, naming the take, frame, row, beat and both names, with the edit that fixes it (`edit --frame <frame_id> --set subject_blocking.N.cast_id=cast_…`, or `--set story_moment="…"` when the listed cast is right). Fix the frame (free), then run the same command again. A redraw that re-authors the frames (`--note`, or a beat edit) is not stopped: it says to check the names on the redrawn board.
- **Every touch names its owner.** A cell where someone grabs, grips, holds or touches someone says whose hand (`right hand`), where that person's body is, and the side of the frame the arm comes from (`frame right`, or `his arm joined to his shoulder`). Three Payments Late t1 row 2 (10-06): "Dez clamps her wrist" on a reverse angle drew a hand from behind Noor attached to no one ($0.30 redraw). The kit prints `!! Before the boards (warning only, nothing stopped): …` after the draft, at the script yes and before a `redraw-board`, naming the take, frame, row and toucher with the free `edit --frame <frame_id> --set subject_blocking.N.interaction="…"` that fixes it; the shot list prints `!! row N: NAME "…" does not say which hand …: look for a hand or arm attached to no one`. A warning, never a stop: the server writes the owner line, the side (flipped on a reverse angle), a `no hand or arm without its visible body` ban, the speaker's open mouth, each person's look and "only these people" on every cell when it draws (fictora-drama), and the edit makes it exact.
- **Speaking mouths free.** Nothing over or at a speaker's mouth on a speaking row: no hand, cup or phone at the lips, no covering, wiping or "clearing" the mouth, no `dramatic_gasp` hand (Hanakaze ep 6). The shot list prints `!! ... hides the mouth`; edit the pose to an open gesture, then redraw.
- **An expression on every emotional row.** A row whose beat ends on an `end_state` or pays off a `satisfaction_type`, or that has a reaction cell or a close-up on a face, should wear a `reaction_kind`: the take prints it as a picture of the face that the provider rewrite keeps; without one the emotion rides the motion text, which the rewrite thins. The shot list prints `!! row N: an emotional moment ... with no expression picked` (warning only); give the beat one with `edit --episode N --beat B --expression KIND` and redraw, or say yes as is.
- **Faces after the hook.** No two insert cells in a row, and at most one insert on a take with a line (ep 6: two countertop inserts right after the hook). The shot list prints `!! N insert cells in a row`.
- **Moods carry from row to row.** A face keeps the mood it came in with: rage, then a calm "hmm" face, then rage again is a reset, not a reaction (Hanakaze ep 7 row 3: "her finger falters" was drawn calm between two rows of rage). The shot list prints `!! rows A→B: ... expression jumps` (or `mood bounces`) from the frames' expression fields. If the row is a reaction on top of the mood, edit its pose or kind (`edit --frame N --set ...`); if the beat really changes the mood, put the cause on screen and name it (`--set expression_cause="..."`, server with the matching fictora-drama change). Edits are free; redraw before paying for the take.
- **Speaking mouths.** No clench, grit, pressed or closed mouth on a speaking row. The big acting goes before the line and after it; during the words the mouth moves. Lips move on every row that carries a line; check the speaker's face is readable there.
- **Unseen movers.** Something that must never be seen moving (a statue, a doll) is banned by name in `forbidden_elements` ("no statue motion") on every frame that shows it. Then each row that shows it opens with it already in place and keeps it in the same place in both cells. The take compile enforces this, but still watch the take for a slide-in.
- **Stated moves.** A move the beat states (the statue now at arm's length) is drawn nearer in the next row that shows it, never in the same place. The server redraws only a position copied on the same setup and logs the rest, so check it by eye. On a `punchy` horror take the threat gets its own insert or point-of-view row, nearer the lens than last seen.
- **Expressions** fit the moment and the right face (library in reference.md).
- **Extreme close-ups** of eyes or skin drift toward photo-real on a drawn look. The note for that row names the show's style ("row 1 only: flat anime, clean ink lines, no photographic texture").
- **One person, twice (Turbo).** On the default lane the video model gets one picture, the board, and draws from every panel of it. The same person drawn in several panels (kneeling at the end of row 3, standing at the start of row 4) can come out as two of them in one shot, and look-alike extras read as the same face. The shot list prints `!!` when one character is in the cells either side of a cut in plainly different postures (standing, kneeling, sitting, lying, crouching, as the frame's pose says them; other poses are not guessed at). Warning only: watch that cut in the take.
- **Extras:** two unnamed people with the same face, or with the lead's stencil, is a board fault: redraw with a note giving each their own face, hair, build and stencil. Post cannot remove a duplicated person from a take.
- **Redraws.** Notes describe shapes, never judgements ("short, round, two clumsy pleats", not "crooked"). To change who is in a shot, edit the frame's staging: `edit --frame N --set 'subject_blocking=[{"cast_id": "cast_hana", "frame_position": "…", "pose": "…", "gaze": "…", "interaction": "…"}, …]'` (every person in the shot, one entry each); the kit sends the whole brief and `cast_refs` to match. To only take someone out, `--set 'cast_refs=["Hana"]'` (names or ids; the server keeps the others' staging, fictora-drama #498). Someone only heard in the take stays out of the shot until their line is back on screen. On a deploy older than #498 only the `subject_blocking` edit works. After an exit, name the destination with their back to the portal. A plate or board refused by image moderation (more often with a child) is reported with its job id, never retried blind.
- The hook, the hand-off, the safe zones, no readable text or digits. Report brightness: information only, a dark board is the human's call. To change a board, follow "Fixing a board" below. When the human names a beat's shots, set them on the beat: `edit --beat N --shot "size|subject|camera|angle"` (repeat, 1–4; shot 1 is the beat's first row), then `redraw-board` if the board is already drawn. Never write "SHOT n —" into `--intent`. When the human names a beat's expression ("she freezes"), set it: `edit --beat N --expression freeze` (`expressions --desk D --episode N` lists the kinds and each beat's request; `none` clears), then `redraw-board` if the board is already drawn. How the speaker plays a beat's line is `edit --beat N --set delivery=KIND`, one of laughing, excited, whispered, shouted, through_tears, deadpan, trailing_off, breathless, cold (`null` for plain); the kit checks it against the deploy's list before sending and names the nearest on a typo. The shot list prints each request and `!!` when no row on the board wears it.

### Fixing a board

A `redraw-board --note` is at most 500 characters (counted after whitespace collapses, as the server counts); a longer one is refused on the desk with its length, nothing sent or booked: cut it and run it again. `redraw-plate --note` takes up to 2000. `--note` does what the app does: the human's complaint goes to the director (`POST /v1/spines/{id}/director/turns`, scoped to the take's beats), its beat edits for that take are applied (the cascade after the script gate, paid items off), the kit prints each changed row (`was` / `now`), then redraws; the server re-authors the take's frames from the edited beats, and the kit prints the rows that changed in the redraw. If the note changed no shot, it stops before paying (nothing drawn). On a server whose regenerate route takes `note` (the kit reads `/openapi.json`, and tries the note on the redraw when that cannot be read), the note rides on the redraw instead: the server re-authors the take's frames from it and draws the board with it as a correction, so even an unchanged shot list draws differently. A server that refuses the field (422 naming `note`) gets it as shot edits through the director instead, under a fresh key, and the kit says so. When the server takes the note it may draw nothing: the kit prints `!! tK: the board was NOT redrawn and nothing was charged` with the reason; nothing is booked and the old board stays. When the reason is `note_needs_detail` it prints the server's question: put it to the human, then `redraw-board --take tK --note "<their answer>"`. When the note changed a beat's shot plan, the kit says so and saves the spine again. A note counts as a change: no `--same-shots` with one. `--cause` is only a label (defaults to the note). Without a note, `redraw-board` stops with `!!` (exit 2, nothing sent or paid) when nothing the board is drawn from changed since it was last drawn: the frame briefs, the take's beats (intent, direction, shot plan, who is in them), the look notes, the cast plates, or a server stale mark from a cascade. Line words alone do not count.

1. First choice: `redraw-board --desk D --episode N --take tK --note "the human's words, as the camera should see them"` (e.g. "medium two-shot walking down the hallway, waist-up, no map"). Show the human the printed per-row changes and the new board. If it stopped because the note changed no shot (or the server drew nothing and said why), rephrase it as what the camera sees, or do steps 2–5 by hand. A question from the server goes to the human word for word; their answer is the next `--note`.
2. By hand: read the human's complaint and map it to specific rows and beats: which frame (`edit --frame N`, one row's `visual_brief`: placement, size, angle, blocking, expression), which beat (`edit --beat N --shot …`, `--expression KIND` or `--intent`), or the whole look (`look-note --add`). A wrong face on a plate is `redraw-plate`, not a board edit.
3. Preview each edit and show the human the before and after (after the script gate `edit … --preview` prints the cascade and changes nothing; before it, say the old and new value from `api/spine.json`).
4. Apply the edits after the human's yes.
5. `redraw-board --desk D --episode N --take tK --cause "short label"`. It redraws from the edited frames; after a beat edit the server re-authors the take's frames first. It prints what changed.
6. Look at the new board and check the complaint is gone before the board gate.

Repurposing an episode (a new idea on an episode already authored): its title and summary are updated first (no kit command yet: ask engineering), because a redraw re-authors the frames from the beats and the summary. Every frame on one board shares one location string, and a line cannot move between beats: no error says so.

Before a paid redraw that re-authors the frames (any `--note`, or a beat edit) the kit prints `!! STAGING MAY CHANGE` and the take's rows as they stand: who is in a row, its size and its role can change, and the server has no preview, so the per-row diff comes only after the draw. When the human's complaint is about one row's who, where or size, step 2's `edit --frame N --set …` is free and exact: the redraw then draws that frame as written. A redraw of edited frames alone (no note, no beat edit) prints no warning.

`--same-shots` (same as `--reroll`) only when the drawing was a random miss and the frames are right (a garbled hand, a smeared face): it draws the same direction again for $0.30. A board whose content comes from another episode (another episode's room, cast or story) is a server bug: report it to engineering with the job id; edits do not fix it.

## A take is not done until finish ran; hosted post is off

The take step stops at the raw clip: the model's sound only, no music, no effects, no captions. Hosted post-production is switched off on the API, so the finish runs on this laptop. As soon as the human says Use it, run it without being asked:

```bash
uv run fictora-produce finish --desk D [--episode N] [--take tK] [--take-file F] [--bed-db N] [--duck-db N] [--sfx-adjust "door=-6"] [--line-start S ...] [--line-end S ...] [--no-colour-match] \
  [--mute A-B ...] [--voice FILE@S[@DB] ...] [--cue FILE@S[@DB] ...] [--caption-label TEXT@A-B ...] [--sign-overlay] [--caption-style bold|subtle|plain|none] [--caption-colour yellow|white]
```

It lays the take's sound effects (from `GET /v1/jobs/{take_job}/take-facts`, never the prompt; placed on the shots as filmed, each planned shot change moved to the take's measured cut), the show's music bed (the harness's: the spine's bed or one the server makes, pinned once per show; never a file or description you choose, see **Music is the harness's** below), matches the look to the approved board, mixes at a measured level near −18 LUFS, burns the show's captions (Bold or Subtle, below) and puts the Sokii mark top left. Every step writes a new `take-epNN-tK-<step>-vN.mp4`. Needs ffmpeg with libass. Generated audio (effects, the bed, audition clips, dry lines, Whisper timings) is made on the server by the Drama API operator audio routes (`sfx-cues`, `audio-bed/render`, `voice-auditions/render`, `voice-lines`, `/v1/transcripts`); no provider key ever goes on this laptop and no local file is uploaded (transcripts read the take's stored URL). Rate limits and in-progress answers are waited out automatically. If a route refuses (`operator_audio_unavailable`, `budget_cap_exceeded`), `finish` still does the look, mix, captions and mark, reports `NOT DONE` and names the refusal: tell engineering, never add a key. Costs go to `run-notes.md` only; never quote them to anyone else.

- The last line reads `Sound: music ✓ · SFX ✓ · mix ✓ · captions ✓`. `NOT DONE` and exit code **5** mean music, SFX or the mix is missing: do not hand the file over; fix what it names and run `finish` again.
- `SFX ✓ (!! N planned cue(s) not laid)` means the take facts planned a sound the take does not have. The `sfx` line names each one and why (`render failed: wrong shape: sustained cue collapses…`, `starts past the take`). Tell the human which sound is missing before they watch; never call it done without saying so. Cues left out on purpose (`--sfx-adjust …=drop`, a sound note) are listed, not flagged.
- **Locked-voice takes.** A newer server can send a Turbo take's lines, rendered in the locked voices, to the video model as its audio. The take facts then say `soundtrack: target_audio`, and the film step, `take-facts`, `review` and `finish` print `Soundtrack: locked voices, N line(s) (no native ambience)`. The transcript says whether a line was heard; the dialogue track says where: a line whose transcript words reach into its window starts where the track's own voice starts there, so Whisper stretching a first word back over the silence before it no longer reads `heard at 4.35s`); the bed ducked 12 dB exactly inside each line window and each laid voice window (`--duck-db` overrides the depth), the effects on the cuts measured on the take (snapped up to 2 s from the plan; every measured cut is printed), and captions timed on the line windows (no transcript). The summary names the ambience (`ambience: "<description>", -28 dB …`), and the sound line gains `ambience ✓` (or `room tone ✓` on the fallback). With no bed or no room tone it prints `!! STOPPED`, writes no mix, captions or mark, and exits **5**: a voice-only take is never handed over. `Soundtrack: native (reason: …)`, or no soundtrack line from an older server, finishes exactly as before.
- **Music is the harness's.** The kit never lays music the operator picks: no pinned file of your own (`set-bed` is gone and only says how to ask for a change), no music description. A bed pinned by hand on an older desk is ignored (`!! ignored the hand-pinned …`). To change the music, say what should change: `uv run fictora-produce music-note --desk D [--episode N] [--take tK] "calmer"` (or `finish --music "calmer"`, the same note). Only takes filmed afterwards play a change (read the human the notice it prints); for takes already filmed, a `music-note` is the way. **Desks created before 6 Oct 2026** (`rules-epoch`) keep the music they were made with: no blend line, and `music-blend --set`/`--default` are refused before anything is sent (the server refuses a pre-6-Oct story too: `409 music_blend_rules_legacy`).
- A `409 hosted_post_off`, or a `503 restate_unavailable` from post-production, is not an outage: never retry it, run `finish`.
- Captions are always the English line. On a Japanese or Korean show, `finish` / `caption` show each whole English line over its speech (no word flicker); the run note says `whole English lines`.
- On such a show each whole line is timed on the **words a transcript of the take heard for it**, not on speech spans: the saved `takes/take-epNN-tK-*words-vN.json` (from `review --transcribe`), else `finish` asks the server for one from the take's stored URL (`/v1/transcripts`, a few cents). A stammer (`も、`) or a mid-line pause no longer starts the caption early or ends it early. Each line stays up at least max(1.2 s, 0.3 s a word), never into the next line. A line the transcript did not hear, or a take changed by hand (`--voice`/`--mute`, or a `--take-file` after `trim`/`tempo`/a sound step or of unknown origin), is timed on speech spans as before.
- An **English** show is timed on a saved transcript too (`review --transcribe` first; `finish` never pays for one on an English take), so a stray mumble or a made-up line no longer pushes later captions onto the wrong words. With no transcript it is timed on speech stretches in order and prints **`EXTRA SPEECH: … not captioned: a-b s`** when stretches are left over: watch the captions, then transcribe, rebuild the take's audio when the stray speech is wrong dialogue (Hand sound, below), or set `--line-start` / `--line-end`.
- Captions come from the **current spine** (read from the server on every `finish` / `caption`, saved as `spine.json`): a line deleted before filming is never burned. `!! captions from the desk's copy …` means the server could not be read: `spine --refresh` and finish again. A `--take-file` made from the raw take only by `freeze`, `soften`, `deboard` or `colour` keeps its sound timeline, so the raw take's transcript still times it (`finish` follows `epNN/takes/edit-chain.jsonl`, or the run notes on an older desk; the report prints the chain). The `captions` report line and the run note say what timed each line: `(words)`, `(speech)`, `(manual)` or a mix like `(words start, manual end)`.
- On a 30 s or 60 s episode each take is captioned with **only the lines of the beats it plays** (split as the board and `check-lines` split them, from the spine's storyboard pattern); `caption --take F` does the same for a `take-epNN-tK-…` file and captions every line on a file not named for one take (a joined episode). If the split cannot be read (an older desk with no pattern, or a pattern that does not match the desk's takes), the take gets every line as before and the report prints `TAKE LINES: …`: check those captions by eye.
- Caption still off? `--line-start S` and `--line-end S`, once per line in order (either or both), on `finish` and `caption`. A hand end is exact: no hold, no reading minimum.
- **Caption style: Bold or Subtle, one choice per show (user decision, 6 Oct 2026).** **Bold** (`bold`, a new show's default): one short line at a time (two or three words, split above ~12 characters, never across a clause mark or a breath), Arial Bold 92 on a 1080×1920 canvas (1.44× the old size; 64 on a 1344-high take), white with ONE word in house yellow `#FFE500`, outline 7 and a heavier shadow, the line's bottom at 76% of the frame. Each word appears when it is said (never more than 80 ms early): the chunk is laid out whole and its unsaid words are transparent, so it never moves as it fills. The yellow word is the line's writer-marked emphasis when the spine gives one (the optional `emphasis_word` on a dialogue line), else the chunk's main word (the last content word, an ALL-CAPS shout first, never a function word; a one-word chunk is yellow only when the word carries meaning). A chunk too wide is set smaller, never wrapped. **Subtle** (`subtle`; `house` is its older name and still works): the yellow Arial Bold 64 word flicker (up to three words build up, then reset), bottom at 62%, wrapped onto two balanced lines when long. A voice heard, not seen (a line marked `off_screen`, any line of a `voice_only` cast member, an inner-voice thought, or a line laid with `finish --voice` that is not a script line, such as narration) keeps Georgia italic in either style, at that style's size, colours and place (in Bold: white with its yellow word, chunked the same way). Never re-burn a kit caption at another size. A letterbox show keeps its own caption band whichever style is set.
- **Asked once, at the look approval, never blocking.** The first time a NEW show's look is approved (`approve --gate look`; on a desk that drew no look frame, its first plates yes) the kit draws a free still, `shared/look/caption-preview-vN.png`: the approved look frame (else the first plate, else the first board) with the show's first dialogue line in Bold (left) and Subtle (right). Show it and say "Captions default to Bold; reply 'subtle' to switch." `caption_style: bold` is saved in `production.config.json` at once, so silence is Bold. On "subtle": `caption-style --desk D --set subtle`. A continuing show (a desk with finished episodes) is not asked: it keeps `subtle`, the captions its episodes went out with (saved the same way), unless the human switches (`caption-style --desk D --set bold`). A desk whose `caption_style` is already set (older desks saved `house`) is never asked, and neither is a desk created before 6 Oct 2026 (it keeps `house`). `caption-style --desk D` prints the show's style and why.
- **No em or en dash in any burned caption** (user rule, 6 Oct 2026). The script and the voice keep their dashes (the voice reads one as a cut-off); what is drawn on the picture does not: a dash ending a cut-off line or chunk is an ellipsis (`Please—` shows `Please…`, in word flicker too), a dash between words is a comma (`D-9341 — do not` shows `D-9341, do not`), an opening dash is dropped. Em, en, `--` and a spaced ` - `, spaced or not; a hyphen inside a word (D-9341, well-known) stays. It applies to `finish` / `caption` captions, inner-voice captions, reel captions, the hook line and title bar, and the letterbox title band, after timing (word timings are unchanged); fictora-drama's server captions follow the same rule. Sign overlays (redrawn lettering in the shot) are not captions and keep the sign's text. Never type a dash into a caption by hand either.
- `--caption-style bold|subtle|plain|none` on `finish`, `caption` and `reel` overrides the show's style for one run: `plain` is white whole lines (Subtle's size and band), `none` no captions (the take is still complete). The show's style is `caption_style` in `production.config.json` (above).
- **Letterbox shows (4:3 takes) get their 9:16 file from `finish` and `join`** (founder decisions, 6 Oct 2026; layout matches the "Not Home" reference; the server uses the same numbers). Black 1080x1920 canvas, the picture at 1080x810 in the middle; the Sokii mark in the top black band (never on the picture); the title block (Not Home's 56 px Arial Bold) just above the picture: the setup line white (the series title), the hook line house yellow (`--hook-line`, else the `hook-line` pick, else the spine's); captions (Not Home's 56 px Arial Bold, one line preferred, centred on the frame unless that would cross x 950) in the band under the picture, yellow by default (`--caption-colour white`, or `letterbox_caption_colour` in the desk config), in phrases of up to five words with no word left alone, ending when the voice ends on a locked-voice take, upright when the take draws a speaker whose line is flagged off screen. `join` puts the mark and title on the joined file once (`--hook-line` / `--no-hook-line` there too). `reel` cuts the same 9:16 letterbox file from the 4:3 takes (mark, title block, band captions in the colour the take was finished with), and `review` reads only the picture on a letterbox file (its opening, frames, cuts, board and tail checks never measure the black bands). The 4:3 takes stay on the desk. Never build the 9:16 file by hand. Portrait shows are untouched. Full table: [reference.md](reference.md) → Finish → Letterbox deliverable.
- **System panels** (system and game genres only: `system_leveling`, `last_human`, `isekai`, `cultivation`, `regression_revenge`): the writer's status windows, level ups, quest notices and gate warnings on beats (`beats[].system_panels`, look `system_panel_look`). `finish` draws them after the captions with a typewriter reveal, in the picture and out of the safe zones; `--no-panels` skips them. Never choose or rewrite one for the creator. Full rules: [reference.md](reference.md) → System panels.
- **Choosing the hook line** (the big text on the first ~3 s, or a letterbox show's yellow title line): `hook-line --desk D --episode N --list` (free, no call) shows the writer's options; `--pick K`, `--text "..."` or `--off` stores the human's choice on the server where the app reads it (free). Read the options to the human and let them pick: a line that spoils the twist is a real fault. On a letterbox show, `--setup-line "..."` (60 characters at most) sets the episode's own white title line (`--default-setup-line` goes back to the series title), alone or with a hook-line choice. On an older server the pick is kept on the desk for `finish` and `join`, and the app still shows the server's.
- **Inner voice.** Each take lays the thoughts saved with `inner-voice` that start in it (step `inner-voice`). Cue times are seconds on the episode as filmed: take N starts at the sum of the raw lengths of the takes before it (ffprobe). A cue past a seam stays on the take where it starts, with `!!`. The dry line is made on the server in the thinker's locked voice (`voice-lines`, same key and ledger unit as `voice-line`; reused from `epNN/voices/` on a re-run, never paid twice), laid at the cue like `--voice` (bed, effects and, on a locked-voice take, the ambience duck under it) and captioned in Georgia italic where it plays. A thought spoken in the show's language under an English caption: `inner-voice --cast X --text "<English caption>" --spoken-text "<the words said>" --at S` (as `voice-line --text/--spoken-text` and `line --spoken/--subtitle`). The server's cue holds one `line`, the caption; the spoken words are kept on the desk in `epNN/inner-voice-spoken.json` (for that cue id and that caption only), listed as `[says: …]`, and `finish` sends them to the voice and captions the English line in Georgia italic. A thought whose `--text` is not English is named (`!! … plays uncaptioned`) by `inner-voice` and by `finish`. Keep the desk file with the desk: without it the thought is voiced as its caption. No locked voice, a refusal, a line past the take's end, an earlier take not on the desk or a cue after the last take: the rest finishes, the cue is named, `inner voice ✗`, `NOT DONE`.

## Hand sound: stray speech, a new line, a missing cue

All three are mix notes, never a re-film. Times are seconds on the take **as filmed** (the raw take): `deboard` keeps the timeline, so nothing is shifted. Each flag repeats.

- **Wrong take dialogue** (garbled fake speech between lines, a stray mumble, a line said wrong): never mute a range. A muted range leaves a hole in the background (the take's own room sound drops out). Drop the take's audio and rebuild it: a `voice-line` for every line (below), an ambience `cue` the length of the take ("<the place> ambience, no music, no speech"), then `finish --mute 0-<take length> --voice FILE@S … --cue AMBIENCE@0`; the effects and the bed are laid as usual. The kit has no one-flag way yet (backlog). A locked-voice take needs none of this: its audio is only the locked lines, and `finish` lays the ambience and bed.
- **A new or changed line:** `voice-line --desk D --cast X --text "..." [--episode N] [--spoken-text "..."]` makes one dry line in X's locked voice on the server ($0.10 per 1,000 characters, Whisper-checked) into `epNN/voices/`. Play it to the human, then rebuild the take's audio as above with the new line among the others (never `--mute` just the old line's range). It is levelled to −18 LUFS unless `@DB`; the bed ducks under it like speech. A line that would run past the take is refused.
- **A sound the take's Sound lines missed:** `cue --desk D --episode N --description "a descending comic brass sting" [--seconds 1.5]` makes one cue on the server (~$0.002 a second) into `epNN/sfx/` and prints its RMS per half second and shape check. Then `finish --cue FILE@S[@DB]`: −8 dB under the take unless `@DB`, 10 dB lower while someone speaks, clamped to end 0.15 s before the take does. A hand cue on a visible action goes with the drop for the same auto sound (`--cue …door-slam…@5.25 --sfx-adjust "door=drop"`), or the door slams twice; `finish` prints `!!` with the drop to use when a hand cue lands within about 1 s of an auto cue of the same sound.
- **Impacts and sound notes (server).** With fictora-drama #462 live, the take facts' cue plan also lays each impact the story's action states (a beat's `end_state`, a row's `chain`, `ends` or `sound`: "a dry stone crack", "the door slams shut") once, as a short cue on the shot that shows it. With #467 live, a sound note on the story (`POST /v1/spines/{id}/sound-notes`) can add one sound to one take ("add a dry stone crack at the end of episode 1 take 2 row 4"); an add note that names no take is `422 sound_note_needs_take`. Both reach only take facts fetched after them: for a take already filmed, `take-facts --refresh` reads its facts again (the sound-note bullet below).
- One cue per visible action, on the frame where the action lands. A cue with a bad shape is rendered once more with a changed description, then dropped with a line in the run notes; a missing cue never blocks a take. A quiet cue: raise it in 2–3 dB steps with `@DB` until it reads.
- `finish` checks every hand file before any step: missing, silent, or outside the take is an error and nothing is written. A hand step that then fails prints `NOT DONE` like missing music.
- **A sound the human wants added to a take** ("add a dry stone crack at the end"): `sound-note --desk D --episode N --take tK [--shot S | --row R] "add a dry stone crack at the end"` saves it on the story (free); on a take plain words work and one note may name several sounds ("a quick blush puff, and a tiny sweat-drop drip"), up to 8 added sounds per take and 16 per episode (fictora-drama #481). A take already filmed keeps its saved facts until `take-facts --desk D --episode N --take tK --refresh` reads them again (a new version; the old file stays) and prints the cues it adds; then `finish` again. `finish` prints `!!` when its facts are older than the sound notes. The server's refusal is printed by name with the fix (`sound_note_needs_take`: pass `--take`). A drop or level note (`sound-note --desk D "no purring"`, "the door slam is too loud") reaches every take the same way: with fictora-drama #475 the take facts leave the dropped cue out and give a levelled cue its dB offset, and `finish` lays that plan (never applying the note a second time); a take already filmed needs `take-facts --refresh` first, which prints each level change (`~`) and drop (`-`) with its note id. `finish --sfx-adjust "door=-6"` / `"hum=drop"` stays as a change for one take only, on top. A sound no note can place (a sting, a wordless making take) is a hand cue. Full cue guide: [sound-cues.md](../../../docs/content-ops/sound-cues.md).
- Ducking target: about 8–9 dB under the voice. When a line must sit clearly over the bed, pass `--duck-db 9`. Never loudnorm a take.
- **Bed level.** The bed's level in the mix is, first that exists: `finish --bed-db N`; the desk's `series.json` `bed_db` (the show's chosen level: set it once and every `finish`, `join` and `reel` uses it); else measured: `finish` reads the bed file's loudness and sets the gain so the bed lands at about −27 LUFS in the mix, about 9 dB under the −18 LUFS dialogue (voices near −16 dB RMS sit about 10 dB over it between lines), clamped to −24..+12 dB. The `bed` step prints the level and why (`-6.4 dB (measured: the bed reads -20.6 LUFS …)`, `(the desk's series.json bed_db)`, `(--bed-db)`), the finish record keeps it (`bed_db`, `bed_db_source`), and a level that puts the bed outside −29.5..−24.5 LUFS gets a `!!` line with the measured level to pass instead. The duck under each line is unchanged. Before this, every finish used a fixed −16.5 dB: on the −20.6 LUFS show bed the music sat near −37 dB, near-silent (Hanakaze, 18 of 18 finishes).
- `finish` saves the mix buses beside the mix (`take-epNN-tK-mix-vN-{raw,ducked,key}-bus.wav`, mono 48 kHz, about 0.3 MB a second together): `review` measures the duck depth from them.
- A take that will be re-filmed gets no `finish`, no cues and no voice lines.

## Review a take: the numbers before the verdict

```bash
uv run fictora-produce review --desk D [--episode N] [--take tK] [--take-file F | --file F] [--board B] [--words-json J | --transcribe] [--json]
```

Free and local (ffmpeg + numpy); only `--transcribe` asks the server for a Whisper transcript of the take's stored URL (a few cents). Without a file it reads the newest finished file for the take, else the newest raw take, and the block's first line says which. Run it on the raw take as soon as it lands (before `finish`, that is the default), before asking Use it or Change this, and again after `finish` before handing it over. It prints one block (and appends it to `run-notes.md`): paste it into the verdict. Each section is ✓ or ⚠ with the threshold it used (`–` when there was nothing to measure). It always exits 0: a ⚠ is something to watch for, never a stop, and never by itself a reason to re-film.

- **Loudness:** a raw take below −30 LUFS needs cues; a finished one sits at −20 to −15 LUFS with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Duck depth (6–12 dB under the voice) from the mix buses `finish` saves; a take finished before this kit wrote them reads `not measured`: finish it again.
- **Cuts:** hard cuts vs the take facts' shot changes. An extra cut is a soften; a missing one is often a camera move: look.
- **Frames:** frozen stretches (a stall, or your own `freeze`) and stacked double frames: look at each full size.
- **Board:** head board frames on a raw take are what `finish` removes; any board frame mid-take, or at the head after deboard, is a fault.
- **Lines:** the `check-lines` output as it prints it (the count, each line's shot and board row, `!!` for an on-screen speaker out of frame), then each approved line heard, `MISSING` (the one real re-film) or, for a line under three words, `CHECK BY EAR` (listen; never a re-film on the transcript alone). Cast names match sound-alike spellings. A Japanese line matched on the server's readings reads `(by sound, NN%)`.
- **People:** the head count per shot the take facts expect (`On screen: 2 people (Hana, + 1 unnamed)`; the same lines print after filming and in `take-facts`). Nothing is detected: count the people in each shot by eye (and on the zone sheet of a finished file), and watch the cuts where a character changes pose. The same person on screen twice is a re-film with the cause `same person rendered twice at shot N`. An older server sends no head counts: the section says so in one line; count by eye anyway.
- **Safe zones** (finished files only): the caption box on sampled frames vs the covered zones and the 55–70% band; it writes a zone sheet (covered zones shaded) for the face check by eye.
- **Opening** (take 1 of every episode, and every reel; `finish` prints the same ⚠ lines for take 1): a dark frame 0 (luma under 0.10 on frame 0 and over the first 0.5 s), a static first second (mean frame-to-frame change under 0.006), and no face on frame 0 (two local OpenCV detectors, real faces and anime/manhwa faces, unioned, the anime one first on an anime/manhwa show; the take facts' head count only when OpenCV fails to load, printed as `!! face detector unavailable`). The face check is skipped when the opening beat has no spoken line (a wordless opening on a place or a thing is fine). Warnings, never a stop: read them to the human. `join` prints them for the joined episode too.
- **Ending** (reels; `join` prints it for the joined episode): more than 0.3 s settled after the last line or action. The cut should land on the peak.
- Not measured: what must not be on screen per board row. Check that by eye. Thresholds and how to read each number: reference.md.

## Local edits: deboard, soften, freeze, trim, tempo, blur

Free, on this laptop (ffmpeg + numpy). Each writes a new `take-epNN-tK-<step>-vN.mp4` and a run note; nothing is overwritten. Details and thresholds: reference.md.

- **Board frames.** A Turbo take opens on a frame or two of the still board. `finish` now removes them first (`deboard`), the only automatic edit to footage: it holds a frame over them and never cuts: measured against the approved board, capped at 12, replaced with the first real frame so the length and sound do not move (take-facts cues and captions keep their times). Those clones are a held still before the first motion, so when the server set no trim handles `finish` cuts them off LAST, like a handle (picture and sound together, captions moved, a `handles` edit with source `kit-deboard` in the record), never past the first line's onset (a line under the board frames keeps its first word; the report says the cut stopped short). None found: nothing written. `--no-deboard` only when the human wants the board opening kept. `CAP HIT` in the report: look at the head at full rate.
- **What the server did at the ends** (`review`, Board section): `server held N start / M end frame(s) · original kept: <url> (sha256 …)` (the provider's untouched take is kept: `review --original` downloads it, checks its sha256 and length, and writes `take-epNN-tK-original-compare-vN.mp4`, held left, original right); `held frames but no original stored (before fictora-drama #559)` on an older take; `!! possible board frames at the start/end (N) left as filmed: <reason>` when the server was not sure. Never call that last one "held 0". The kit does not hold an end the server left as filmed either: `deboard` stops with `Refused` and `finish` skips its deboard with a `!!`. Look at that end with the human; only if it is the board, `deboard --hold-unsure` and `finish --take-file` the result.
- **Order.** raw → `deboard` → `soften` / `freeze` → `finish --take-file <that file>` → `trim` / `tempo` on the finished file → watch.
- `soften` always after `deboard` (the board-to-motion jump reads as a cut). No `--cut` detects hard cuts itself; say which cuts it found.
- `freeze --at S --hold S` keeps the length: the held frame covers the picture, the sound plays on.
- **Trim handles** (fictora-drama #563; the server's, not a file cut): `trim --desk D --episode N --take tK --start S --end E` sets where the take starts and ends when it plays (seconds on the take as filmed, the take-facts times; at least 1 s; snapped to a frame, said when it snaps); `--reset` puts back the automatic handles (just past the board frames the server held); `--preview` shows the change and sends nothing. It prints the handles now and new, what each end loses, and a `!!` for every line or effect the window cuts, then ends on `Applied` or `Refused: <reason>` (a named server code gets its fix). Nothing is billed and no file is cut; it saves the take facts again. Needs the producer's yes like any trim. `finish` then lays everything on the take as filmed and cuts at the handles LAST (the take before the bed, the master, the marked file, the captions' `.ass`; a new finish record with a `handles` edit), so `join` joins the take at its handles like the server; `join`, `review` and `reel` read take-facts times on such a file minus `start_s`. A trim made in the app reaches `finish` after `take-facts --refresh`. Handles that are the whole take cut nothing; an older server sends no handles and nothing is cut.
- `trim --cut` only on the finished file (`--take-file` is required), and only after the producer's yes: no trim or cut of footage without it. Show what was cut (the times and the frames either side). Check the first frame after the cut at full size. A finished cut shorter than its band (e.g. 14.2 s from a 15 s episode) is allowed: say the new length; it is not a deviation.
- `tempo --factor 0.9` only for a show cut slow, and only on the finished file: `finish` lays effects at the filmed times.
- Keep the un-marked master (`…-cap-vN.mp4`); the marked `…-sokii-vN.mp4` goes out. Previews, rough cuts and "just combine" files are made from finished takes and carry the house captions too.
- An overlay added in post (a `blur` box, a title) never covers the key reveal: render test frames of the key shots first and look at them.
- `blur --box x,y,w,h --from S --to S` is a **patch** for garbled readable text the video invented (a sign reading "…nakaze Swettes", a shop name that is not a word) when re-filming is not worth it. Run it on the finished file. Boxes are pixels of the take (top-left x, y, width, height; frame-grab the moment to measure; must lie inside the frame); cover the whole sign with a margin and the whole time it is readable. Hard edge by default: a feather that fades INTO the box leaves text readable, so `--feather PX` only softens outside the box. Watch the window at full size. Always report it (learnings row + the reply): it hides the symptom, the server should stop making the text.

```bash
uv run fictora-produce deboard --desk D [--episode N] [--take tK] [--take-file F] [--board B]
uv run fictora-produce soften  --desk D [--take-file F] [--cut S ...]
uv run fictora-produce freeze  --desk D [--take-file F] --at 6.2 --hold 0.6
uv run fictora-produce trim    --desk D --take-file FINAL --cut 10.17-12.15 [--cues-json J]
uv run fictora-produce trim    --desk D --episode N --take tK --start 0.5 --end 10 [--preview] | --reset
uv run fictora-produce tempo   --desk D --take-file FINAL [--factor 0.9]
uv run fictora-produce blur    --desk D --take-file FINAL --box 0,208,178,234 [--box ...] --from 2.38 --to 6.62 [--strength 20] [--feather 0]
```

On a finished file with the cover attached these keep the cover and write `…-<step>-cover-vN.mp4`.

## Join: the 30 s / 60 s episode and the series cut

Free, on this laptop. When an episode has a second take, offer the join as soon as every take is finished; a series cut is the same command across episodes.

```bash
uv run fictora-produce join --desk D --episode N                  # every take of N, newest finish each, in take order
uv run fictora-produce join --desk D --episodes 1 2 3             # series cut -> shared/cuts/
uv run fictora-produce join --desk D --take-file F1 --take-file F2   # these finished takes, in this order
uv run fictora-produce join --desk D --take-file T1-sokii --take-file T2-recap --from-record T2-cap   # a re-captioned copy stands in for its recorded master
uv run fictora-produce join --desk D --episode N --ending freeze-black   # optional: hold the last frame, then black
```

- It reads the record `finish` leaves (`take-epNN-tK-finish-vN.json`): each take's sound before the bed and its un-marked captioned picture. So there is **one music bed across the whole join** (the harness bed pinned on the desk, looped seamlessly; there is no flag for a file of your own), never each take's bed stitched; takes that carry the harness's music in their own soundtrack join with no bed, and a join mixing those with bedded takes is refused; captions ride on their picture; the mark goes on **once**, on the joined file. The un-marked master stays beside it.
- Takes of one episode meet on a straight cut (they hand off on the same frame); episodes meet on a 0.25 s dissolve. `--dissolve S` sets every seam; say so when you use it.
- Each take gets one gain to the takes' median level, then one measured mix gain and one limiter. No loudnorm.
- The bed's level across the join: `--bed-db`, else the desk's `series.json` `bed_db`, else the level every take was finished at when one was chosen (a flag or the desk; the old unchosen −16.5 default is not carried), else measured from the bed as `finish` does. `join` prints `Bed level: …` with why.
- Refused before anything is written: a take that is not finished (every take on the desk needs a complete finish), a take not at 24 fps, mixed sizes. The joined file is counted at 24 frames a second.
- Each seam's room-level step is printed. Over 5 dB: `NOT DONE`, exit **5**, the master is kept to listen to and nothing is marked; do not hand it over.
- `trim` / `tempo` (and `freeze` / `soften` / `blur`) on a finished file apply the same edit to the take before the bed and the un-marked master and write a new finish record (the edit chain in `edits`), so the edited take joins at its new length; the newest record wins, so `--episode N` joins the edited take. A file no record names (edited from a raw take, or while a record file was gone: the edit says `No finish record`) is refused: finish again, then edit. Never join the -sokii files by hand (that stitches two beds and two marks).
- A take finished before `join` existed has no record: run `finish` on it again before the join.
- A copy of a finished take with only the picture changed (re-captioned) has no record either. Pass `--from-record <the -cap master it was made from>` after its `--take-file` (the Nth `--from-record` goes with the Nth unrecorded `--take-file`). `join` checks it has the same size, frame count and sound as that master, joins its picture over the record's sound before the bed, and writes `SUBSTITUTED: … stands in for …` in the run notes. A copy of the marked `-sokii` final is refused (it would be marked twice): make the copy from the `-cap` master.

- **Shape.** Opens on a 1-2 s flash-forward from the pivot or the peak before the reveal (the planner never takes it from the new fact, the last beat). ## Show voices: locked or the model's own (one choice per show)

Whose voices a show's takes speak in is the show's choice, kept on the server (founder decision, 5 Oct 2026), so a season never changes voice actor between episodes:

- **locked** (the default for a new show): each line is rendered in the character's locked voice (cast card) and the take films to that track. The Voices gate applies, and `finish` lays no bed when the server baked the show's music into the track.
- **model**: the video model voices each character from the cast card's voice description, as every take did before 2 Oct. No Voices gate (there is no locked voice to hear), and `finish` lays the show's own theme as on those episodes: the kit films a model-voice show with `music_by_finish`, so the video model is asked for no music.

A show that never chose keeps the voices its filmed episodes used: any episode filmed with the model's own voices keeps the show on `model`. `step`, the estimate and `film` print `Voices for the next take: …` with the reason; `voice-mode --desk D` prints it on its own (free).

**Ask at the start of every new episode on a continuing show.** Before `author` / `brief` for episode 2 on (and when you pick up or bind a desk that already filmed episodes), run `uv run fictora-produce voice-mode --desk D`. When it says the video model's own voices (earlier episodes filmed with them, or set for the show), ask the human, word for word:

> Keep the same voices as earlier episodes (recommended) or switch to locked voices?

- Keep (the default; also when the human does not choose): `uv run fictora-produce voice-mode --desk D --set model`. It stores the choice so it never moves; there is no Voices gate.
- Switch: only on the human's explicit say-so, `uv run fictora-produce voice-mode --desk D --set locked`. Read them the notice it prints (earlier episodes keep their voices, so the voices change from this episode on), then run the Voices gate before filming.

Never change a show's voice mode yourself, and never to make a gate go away. A show already on locked voices is not asked.

**Re-film an episode in the voices of earlier episodes** (an episode filmed in locked voices on a show whose earlier episodes used the model's own, such as Beach Court After Dark episode 4), each step after the human's yes:

```bash
uv run fictora-produce voice-mode --desk D                       # free; says which voices the next take uses and why
uv run fictora-produce voice-mode --desk D --set model           # free; the show's new takes use the model's voices (no Voices gate)
uv run fictora-produce film --desk D --episode N --cause "re-film in the voices of episodes 1 to N-1"   # prices it; check the Voices line says the video model's own voices
uv run fictora-produce film --desk D --episode N --cause "re-film in the voices of episodes 1 to N-1" --confirm-spend   # after the human's yes to the number
uv run fictora-produce finish --desk D --episode N --take tK     # each take; then join for a 30 s / 60 s episode
```

The takes filmed before keep their voices on the server and stay on the desk (new `-vN` files). One take only: `film --desk D --episode N --take tK --cause "…"`, same voice-mode step first.

## Change a character's voice (never regenerate)

"The voice feels off" is a voice change, not a new story and not a new video. Never re-draft or re-film everything for it. The same commands run the Voices gate before the first take: audition, pick (the pick is the human's yes), or keep the voice as it is.

```bash
uv run fictora-produce voice --desk D --cast NAME --audition [--episode N]   # 4-10 candidates on their real lines, $0.30
uv run fictora-produce voice --desk D --cast NAME --audition --text "…" --voices Rachel,Aria,Sarah   # any wording (≤300 chars), read by exactly those voices
uv run fictora-produce voice --desk D --cast NAME --pick N                   # after the human picks (N or the voice's name); free; records their yes
uv run fictora-produce voice --desk D --cast NAME --keep                     # the human keeps the locked voice; free
uv run fictora-produce voice --desk D --keep-all                             # the human keeps every speaking voice; free
uv run fictora-produce voice --desk D --list                                 # each speaking voice and whether it has a yes; free
uv run fictora-produce revoice --desk D --cast NAME --episode N --take tK   # each filmed take they speak in
uv run fictora-produce finish --desk D --episode N --take tK --take-file <take-epNN-tK-revoice-vN.mp4>
```

After a voice change, `revoice` the takes already filmed that the character speaks in (rules below), then `finish --take-file` on each. Play the listening reel (`shared/voices/<cast>/audition-vN/reel-vN.m4a`; `reel-vN.txt` says where each numbered candidate starts) to the human; a second audition set needs `--cause`. `--text` is any wording up to 300 characters, on the spine or not (on a Japanese or Korean show, written in that language); `--voices` is exactly the Eleven v3 voices the human named, read in that order, one candidate each (`--count` is ignored). Both go to the server as they are; a broken rule comes back as a named refusal before anything is spent (`voice_audition_unknown_voice` lists the catalog; also `_voice_count`, `_text_too_long`, `_text_language`): read it to the human and ask again, never swap a voice yourself. A retired premade (`Bella`) resolves to its catalog stand-in and a repeated name is auditioned once. A set that already holds the wording and the named voices makes a new reel for free. Picking a candidate does not change the script: if the new wording should be said in the episode, `edit --line-id` the line too. On Turbo (the default) a take with `Soundtrack: native` (or none: an older server) does not carry the locked voice, because voice references are not sent: revoice each such filmed take the character speaks in, including takes filmed after the pick. A take with `Soundtrack: locked voices` already speaks in the voice locked when it was filmed: never revoice it for that; only a voice picked after it was filmed needs `revoice --over-locked-voices`, and takes filmed after the pick carry the new voice as they are. Only on R2V do takes not filmed yet use the new voice as they are. Re-film only a take where the dub does not sit (lips visibly wrong, a shouted line), with a cause and a stated cost; never the other takes, never the story.

An off-screen voice (speaker, phone, radio) played over another character's face is heard as that face speaking: give it a source in post with `voice-fx --file F --range A-B [--range C-D ...] --preset intercom|phone|radio` (local ffmpeg, $0, one new file at the same level; repeat `--range` for every line in one call), then `finish --take-file` on it (captions are timed on the revoice words).

`revoice` finds a kana-pinned Japanese line in Whisper's kanji: on the per-word readings the server's transcript carries, or by reading shape on an older server. If a line is still reported "not heard", pass `--words-json` or re-film only that take. A short line (three words or fewer, "Blinking!") the transcript missed is placed on its planned window instead (the soundtrack's line window, else the planned shot timing from the take facts) and printed `!! … used its planned window A-B s`: listen to it. A short line better shown than heard: `finish --caption-label "Blinking!@A-B"` captions it with no spoken line.

## Recovery

- Video stuck ~50% in post: `fictora-produce cancel-job --desk D --job-id job_video_…` and stop. Never `retry-video`, never `--confirm-spend` again.
- A job the server has stopped updating: while any job runs (draft, plates, boards, a redraw, video), the kit watches its `updated_at` and progress. When neither has moved for 10 minutes it prints once (and again every 10 minutes) `WARNING: The server has not updated this job for N min (last update HH:MM UTC). It may be stuck: …` with the `status` and `cancel-job` commands. Slow jobs keep moving; a frozen one is likely stuck on the server. Check `fictora-produce status --desk D`; to stop it, tell the human and `fictora-produce cancel-job --desk D --job-id <id>`. The kit never cancels or retries by itself, and the usual deadlines still hold. Filming (`film`, `step`) watches the take jobs and clips too, and its warning starts `video job job_video_…:`. It also reads `/v1/video-generations/{id}` each poll and stops at once, with the server's code and message, when that route says failed or cancelled even while `/v1/jobs` still says `running 0%`; never `--confirm-spend` again while a film is stuck.
- `cancel-job` answers `terminal: false` when the cancel is only requested: it says the job has not stopped yet and that steps already started on the server may keep running for a while (a server fix is in progress), waits 15 s, reads the job once more and says its status. Do not send the cancel again, and never enrol another take while it runs.
- A redraw, `author` or `rewrite` run again after its saved job was cancelled (`cancel-job`) or ended failed starts a fresh job under the next attempt's key and says `Job … ended cancelled; it is not picked up again` (a redraw re-sends its note). A running, queued or completed saved job is picked up as before and never sent twice.
- Change this on one take (human yes + a written cause): `film --desk D --episode N --take tK --cause "…"` prices that take alone; after the yes, the same with `--confirm-spend` films only it (fresh seed) and books only it. Episodes 1..N−1 and the episode's other takes are never filmed again.
- A whole episode again (rare; human yes + cause): `film --desk D --episode N --cause "…"`, then `--confirm-spend`. `retry-video --new-paid-take` does the same for the desk's current episode, once per desk.
- `film` or `step` says the deployed API does not film one episode alone yet: nothing was sent or charged. Stop and tell engineering; never work around it.
- `info: stranded_voice` before a film: a voice-only character has no lines left. The server films without them and draws no plate (fictora-drama #517); nothing to do. `422 spine_reuse_invalid: cast.<id>.visual_brief is required` on the film means a deploy older than #517 (nothing charged): give them a line back (`line --add --beat N --speaker NAME --text "…" --off-screen`), film again, tell engineering. Never work around it with raw HTTP.
- Interrupted poll: run the same `step` again; the job may still be running.
- Boards refused `422 cast_not_approved` for plates the human already approved (nothing changed): after the human's yes to the same plates, `fictora-produce approve --desk D --gate plates --again` ($0, draws nothing), then `retry-step --desk D --cause "plates re-approved"`, then `step`. `step` prints these commands.
- A dropped connection during `step` or `film`: run the same command again (with the same flags). It sends the same key and the server answers with the job it already has: nothing is charged twice. On desks from 6 Oct 2026 a drop while waiting no longer marks the desk failed; never `retry-step` after a drop, which starts a new paid job.
- A `step` failed (`status` says `failed`; e.g. boards "row board prompt exceeds GPT Image 2 provider limit"): fix the cause first, then `fictora-produce retry-step --desk D --cause "…"`. It backs up `production.json`, puts the failed stage back with a fresh key, says what it will re-run and what that costs (boards $0.30 each), and sends nothing; then `step`. Never hand-edit `production.json`. A failed take goes to `retry-video`/`film`, not here.
- More: reference.md.

## Episode 2 on

Episode 1 approved first. Ask the voices question first (see "Show voices": `voice-mode --desk D`; on a show filmed with the model's own voices, keep them unless the human says switch). Ask the human how many episodes the run should be. For a long run prefer an arc with an engine (a situation that repeats with a new problem) plus a slow question; refuse an arc that closes within a few episodes. Steer each later episode by its direction in the human's words, not by series-wide notes. Never pre-stage scripts for later episodes. Write episode N+1 from the last ~5 s actually filmed and delivered, not from the script. An authored episode cannot be authored again (`author` answers `409 prior_episode_not_approved` / `invalid_extension_ordinal`). To re-write a drafted episode before its script yes, `rewrite --episode N --line "…" [--title T]` (2 on, free, a draft): the line becomes an episode note (it replaces the last `rewrite` direction on that episode; other notes stay), the server re-writes the episode from its notes, and the script prints as after `author`. After the script yes, change it only with `edit` / `line`. Commands: `arc --list [--episodes N]`, `arc --pick K`, `author --episode N --direction K | --line "…"`, then `approve --gate script` and the same loop (reference.md). The arc can be re-picked until episode 2 is written; after that the server answers `409 series_arc_not_open`. Episodes are found by ordinal: new ones are `episode_NN`, older stories keep `ep_02` on, and every command resolves both.

## Adopting a desk from the old internal kit

A desk made by the retired internal kit (raw takes named `take-epNN-tK-vN.mp4`, a desk-root `api/18_takes.json`, no `production.json`) does not open here until it is adopted. Run the plan first and read it with the human:

```bash
uv run fictora-ops adopt-desk --desk D --dry-run   # prints every file it would create and every inferred value
uv run fictora-ops adopt-desk --desk D             # only after the human agrees with the plan
```

It only creates files: `series.pre-adopt.json` (a backup), a hard link `take-epNN-tK-raw-vN.mp4` beside each old raw take, `epNN/api/17_raw_scene_clips.json` and `epNN/api/spine.json`, `production.config.json`, and `production.json` with the episode and phase it inferred. Nothing is renamed, moved, deleted or overwritten, and the take list's `provider_spec` is dropped unread. Running it twice changes nothing. An existing, different `production.json` stops it; `--force` replaces it only after a backup. Every `CONFIRM` line in the plan (a gate the old desk never recorded, an unfinished job, a take without a verdict) goes to the human before the next paid step.

## Learnings: after every video, without being asked

The human makes episodes through this kit only and cannot see the server or app code. Never ask them to open or check it; say how sure you are about where a fault comes from.

**Each run.** When an episode is accepted, abandoned or stops partway, write or update `learnings.md` in the production folder (beside `run-notes.md`, shape from [templates/learnings.md](../../../docs/content-ops/templates/learnings.md)), then add the same items to the running ledger `~/Downloads/documents/fictora-learnings/ledger.md` (create it the first time; save its path to your memory). One item each:

- **ID** `L-YYYYMMDD-n`; **series / episode / stage** (brief, script, cast, look, board, take, finish, captions, sound, export)
- **What happened**, in plain words, and **cause**: `confirmed` (the kit or server said so) or `suspected` (say why). No guesses about model internals.
- **Fix belongs in**: `server` (refused, wrong answer, wrong render), `kit` (a command or script here), `app` (seen in the Sokii app), `runbook rule` (nothing broken; do it differently), or `unsure`
- **Cost** (re-renders, dollars, minutes), **evidence** (job ids, the command, the exact error code; never prompt text), **workaround**
- **Kind**: `bug`, `issue` (works, badly), `fix` (a change that worked), `learning` (a rule for next time)

A learning names the cause and changes what is typed next time. An item already in the ledger is not added again: append "seen again: <series> ep N, <date>" and raise its count. A clean run writes "no new learnings"; never invent items. Then tell the human in two or three lines what you added and ask: "Anything you want to add in your own words?"

**Their own words stay separate.** Anything the human adds, asked or not, goes verbatim into `~/Downloads/documents/fictora-learnings/my-notes.md` with the date and series. Never rewrite or merge it into your items; if it names the same problem, add a `see L-…` link and keep both.

**Offer the composite.** At the start of a session and after each video, count ledger items not marked `shared`. If any: "You have N learnings from M runs since <date> that haven't been shared. Want the composite?" On a yes, write `~/Downloads/documents/fictora-learnings/composite-YYYY-MM-DD.md` (a new dated file, never an overwrite; also publish it as a page if you can publish Artifacts here):

1. Top: repeats first, by count, then anything that cost money
2. Server, Kit, App, Runbook rules, Unsure: bugs before issues
3. Fixes that worked
4. Producer notes (<name>), verbatim
5. Runs covered: series, episodes, dates, re-render cost

After the human says it was sent (write-ups go to the addresses in [README.md](../../../docs/content-ops/README.md)), mark those items `shared: <date>`. An item seen again after sharing is flagged "seen again after sharing". A product gap still gets its [backlog.md](../../../docs/content-ops/backlog.md) line when the series closes; the ledger does not replace it.
